# Launch

Good work with no audience goes nowhere, and loud work with nothing real behind it burns
trust. Make the true value easy to see, for the right people, in their own channels.

## 1. Who and why

From the profile and the project: who it's for (be specific), the pain it removes, and the
proof it works (a demo, numbers, a real user). Write the one-line pitch: *"<what it does> for
<who>, so they <outcome>."* Test it: would that person stop scrolling for it?

## 2. Check before you announce (sw-deep-search)

- **Name:** not taken in the same space; check GitHub, package registries, domains, and
  trademark rules (for example, don't use "Claude" or "Anthropic" in names).
- **Competitors:** what exists, and the honest difference.
- **Channels:** where these people actually are (specific subreddits, HN, X, LinkedIn,
  Discord servers, newsletters, marketplaces and directories).

## 3. Make the assets

- **README / landing:** pitch, a 30-second demo (GIF or screenshot), one-line install per
  platform, what it does and doesn't do, license. Short.
- **Channel posts**, written for each channel's culture: a personal "how I built/use it"
  story for HN and blogs, a short hook plus demo for X/LinkedIn, plain and useful for Reddit
  (no hype, follow each subreddit's self-promotion rules).
- **Listings:** submissions for relevant marketplaces and directories.

Everything public is a `review` card first. Never post, email or submit without a yes.

## 4. Plan and follow up

A short launch plan: order of channels, times, who replies to comments. After launch, track
real signals (installs, stars, issues, replies) for a week and turn the lessons into goals.

## Honesty

No fake reviews, sock-puppet accounts, bought stars or invented numbers. Claims in copy are
held to `sw-verify`'s research standard.
